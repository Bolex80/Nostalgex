Most channel guides treat every viewer in the house as the same person. The same rows appear in the
same order, and the things you actually watch are scattered somewhere inside them. Personal channels
start from the opposite end. They look at what each person has really been playing, then give that
person a channel of their own.

The starting point is the history your server already keeps. Every film and every episode carries a
record of who watched it, so the favourites are not a guess. A channel built this way contains the
series someone keeps coming back to, the films that get played again on a Sunday afternoon, and a
few close neighbours from the same library that fit the same taste. Nothing is fetched from outside
and nothing is uploaded anywhere. The history stays on your own server and the channels are built
from media you already own.

Because the channels are built from your library, they can only contain what is actually there. If a
favourite show has been removed from the server, it cannot be scheduled, and the channel simply
fills itself with the rest of that person's list. When the show comes back, it joins the rotation
again on the next build. This is also why a personal channel sometimes looks smaller than the big
genre channels. It is a careful selection rather than an entire shelf.

Each person gets something slightly different. Someone who lives in long running dramas gets a
channel full of them. A younger viewer gets the animated films they ask for by name, with similar
titles alongside so there is always something familiar to fall into. A teenager with a short history
gets a broader channel in the same spirit, built from the closest matching shows in the library.

The result is a guide that feels like it belongs to the household instead of to the software. Open
it and the first rows are already relevant, without scrolling past a hundred channels that nobody
in the house has ever watched.

Everything stays editable. The lists live in the same channel configuration as every other channel,
so a new favourite can be added, or a title that has fallen out of favour can be dropped, and the
next rebuild picks up the change. There is no account to manage and no recommendation service to
trust, just your own history and your own media, arranged into channels that make sense for the
people who watch them.
