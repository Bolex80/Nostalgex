This bundle sorts your library by who made the thing, which sounds dry until you see your own guide fill up.

Eleven channels, each one gathering the titles in your library that came out of the same studio or the same service. It is the oldest way of organising a TV lineup there is. A channel used to mean a company with a house style, and you knew roughly what you were getting before the title card finished.

That turns out to still be true. Studios have a feel. One channel ends up being prestige drama and one ends up being the big loud summer stuff, not because anybody tagged them that way but because that is what those places actually made. Once your own shelf is split along those lines, you notice how much of your library came from two or three places.

Nothing here streams from a subscription. This is worth saying clearly, because the bundle name confuses people: Nostalgex has no catalogue of its own and no connection to any service. It reads the Plex or Jellyfin library sitting on your server, uses the studio information already attached to those files, and builds channels out of what you own. If you have never bought anything from a particular studio, that channel never appears.

As with every bundle, a channel waits until there is enough on your shelf to fill a day before it turns on, so expect a lopsided lineup. Everyone has a few studios they have quietly collected and a few they have almost nothing from.

Then it behaves like the rest of the app: real schedule, already playing when you tune in, drop into the middle of something rather than starting from a menu.

Free, open source under the MIT license, Apple TV on tvOS 18 or later.
